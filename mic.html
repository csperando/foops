Phony Microphone Detection Playground

A site can tell whether a physical microphone is hearing the room or a virtual audio device (VB-Cable, VoiceMeeter, BlackHole, a voice changer, speech synthesis) is feeding it. Some checks here run without permission. The rest analyze a few seconds of the recording itself: its noise, its bandwidth, and whether any of it repeats exactly.

Cards 1–3 and 11 run without any permission. Cards 4–10 need the microphone, so they wait for this button. The browser's echo cancellation, noise suppression and gain control are turned off, since they'd reshape what the cards measure. Audio is analyzed in this tab and never leaves the page.

2. Device inventory — enumerateDevices()

        checking
        

        

        Lists the audio inputs, leaving out the duplicate "default" and "communications" entries
        Chrome adds. Several inputs are normal (webcam mic, headset) but also what installing virtual
        audio software looks like. Labels stay hidden until microphone access is granted.
        

        

    


    
    

        

        
3. Is getUserMedia being faked?

        checking
        

        

        A script or extension can replace getUserMedia to return synthetic speech or a
        recording built with Web Audio, or fake the Web Audio APIs a detector records through. This
        compares 15 APIs against pristine copies from a hidden iframe.
        

        

    


    
    

        

        
4. Device name: virtual audio names

        not started
        

        

        Matches the active input's name against the defaults of virtual audio cables, voice changers
        and loopback inputs ("Stereo Mix"). Noise-removal apps (Krisp, NVIDIA Broadcast) wrap a real
        mic, so they're noted but not flagged. Easy to beat by renaming the device.

5. Track shape: settings, capabilities, groupId

        not started
        

        

        A capture track has a deviceId from the device list, and the browser offers its audio
        processing on it. A track a script built from Web Audio has neither. A shared
        groupId with a speaker or camera means one physical product.

6. Signal: is there ever true digital silence?

        not started
        

        

        A microphone and its converter always add noise, so even a silent room never yields a stretch
        of samples that are exactly zero. Injected audio usually does, between words.
        

        
(needs microphone)

    


    
    

        

        
7. Signal: bandwidth brick wall

        not started
        

        

        A microphone records up to just under half its sample rate. Speech synthesis, voice changers and
        compressed recordings are made at lower rates and resampled, which leaves a sheer cliff in the
        spectrum (at 8, 11, 12 or 16 kHz) with nothing above it. Bluetooth headsets in call mode are
        genuinely narrowband.
        

        
(needs microphone)

    


    
    

        

        
8. Signal: sample resolution (experimental)

        not started
        

        

        Finds the step size the sample values sit on. 16 bits is normal for microphones and virtual
        cables alike; a grid coarser than any converter means the audio was crushed before it arrived.
        

        
(needs microphone)

    


    
    

        

        
9. Signal: analog traces (hum, DC offset)

        not started
        

        

        Positive evidence only. An analog path picks up 50/60 Hz mains hum and often a small DC offset;
        synthetic audio has neither. Their absence proves nothing (laptops on battery have no hum).
        

        
(needs microphone)

    


    
    

        

        
10. Signal: exact repeats

        not started
        

        

        Fresh noise lands on every sample a microphone records, so no stretch ever repeats bit for bit.
        A looped clip (say, recorded room noise filling the silence) or a generated tone does.
        

        
(needs microphone)

    


    
    

        

        
11. Context signal: is this even physical hardware?

        checking
        

        

        The same WebGL renderer check as the other pages. Inside a VM or remote-desktop session the
        microphone is redirected, so treat the signal cards above with extra caution.
