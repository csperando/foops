Phony Webcam Detection Playground

A site can tell whether a real camera is filming you or a virtual one (OBS, ManyCam, a looped video, a still image) is feeding it. Some checks here run without permission. The rest analyze the live feed itself: timing, pixel noise, and whether it reacts to light from this screen.

1. Permissions API state (no prompt)

checking

navigator.permissions.query({name: "camera"}) reveals whether camera access is already granted, denied or unset — without showing a dialog. 


    


    
    

        

        
2. Device inventory — enumerateDevices()

        checking
        

        

        Lists the video inputs. More than one camera is a weak hint: a laptop with a virtual camera
        installed usually shows its real camera plus the virtual one. Labels stay hidden until camera
        access is granted. Updates live as devices come and go.
        

        

    


    
    

        

        
3. Is getUserMedia being faked?

        checking
        

        

        A script or extension can replace getUserMedia to return a canvas or video
        stream instead of a camera, or fake the canvas APIs a detector reads pixels through. This
        compares 15 APIs against pristine copies from a hidden iframe (which a page-wide
        toString patch can't fake) and, once the camera starts, checks that the track
        really comes from a listed capture device.
        

        

    


    
    

        

        
4. Camera name: virtual camera names + USB ID

        not started
        

        

        Matches the active camera's label against default names used by virtual camera software
        (OBS Virtual Camera, ManyCam, XSplit VCam, Snap Camera, DroidCam, Chrome's fake device…).
        Easy to beat by renaming the driver, but most people don't. Also checks for the USB
        vendor:product ID Chromium appends to real USB cameras' names, which virtual cameras never have.
        

        
(needs camera)

    


    
    

        

        
5. Hardware shape: getCapabilities() + groupId

        not started
        

        

        Real webcams expose physical controls (exposure, focus, white balance, zoom…) and a
        webcam with a built-in mic shares a groupId with it. A virtual camera is just a
        frame source and usually exposes neither.
        

        
(needs camera)

    


    
    

        

        
6. Hardware signal: does the control actually do anything?

        not started
        

        

        Card 5 reads what the camera claims. This drives a brightness or exposure control to its
        minimum and maximum, then restores it, and checks that the picture really darkens and brightens.
        A virtual camera can advertise a control, but nothing happens to its frames.
        

        
(needs camera)

    


    
    

        

        
7. Sensor signal: frame timing

        not started
        

        

        Reads each frame's timestamp via requestVideoFrameCallback. A physical sensor's
        clock jitters and slows in dim light. A software-paced virtual camera ticks almost perfectly
        evenly. Browser scheduling adds jitter to everything, so this is only ever a weak signal.
        

        
(needs camera)

    


    
    

        

        
8. Sensor signal: pixel noise, frozen or looped feed

        not started
        

        

        Real sensors add fresh noise to every frame, and that noise varies with brightness (shadows are
        noisiest). A still image repeats exactly, a looped video returns to an earlier frame far more
        exactly than noise allows, and noise added to a clean image is the same at every brightness.
        

        
(needs camera)

    


    
    

        

        
9. Challenge: does the feed react to this screen?

        not run
        

        

        Flashes the screen through a random red/green/blue sequence and checks whether the color of
        your face in the feed follows it, how likely that is by chance (p-value), how long the camera
        took to react, and whether your face tinted more than the background. A pre-recorded or
        synthetic feed can't know the sequence in advance. This is the strongest check on the page.
        

        

            Face the camera, sit close to the screen, ideally in a dim room. The screen changes color
            twice a second for 5 seconds — skip this if you're sensitive to flashing light.
        

        
        
        
(needs camera)

    


    
    

        

        
10. Cross-check: do the readings agree?

        not started
        

        

        Independent readings of the same camera should match: reported vs. actual resolution, reported
        vs. measured frame rate, the track's name vs. the device list, and what this page's
        getSettings says vs. a pristine copy. Faked streams tend to get one wrong.
        

        
(needs camera)

    


    
    

        

        
11. Session monitor: does it stay real?

        not started
        

        

        The other cards check once. A virtual camera can pass them live and then switch to a recording,
        so this keeps watching every frame for loops (up to 10 minutes long) and freezes, and logs
        mutes, stalls and changes of device or format for the rest of the session.
        

        
(needs camera)

    


    
    

        

        
12. Context signal: is this even physical hardware?

        checking
        

        

        The same WebGL renderer check as the monitor page. Inside a VM or remote-desktop session the
        camera is redirected or emulated, so treat the sensor cards above with extra caution.
